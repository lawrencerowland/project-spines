One-page explanation

One-liner: In a finite schedule, compare a structure-selected spine with the duration-selected critical path: both may qualify as spines because their selection criteria—not mutually exclusive categories—are what differ.

Easy A critical path is a duration story. It tells you which linked tasks currently drive the finish date.
Hard CPM chooses a longest weighted chain in a finite dependency poset. Several such chains may tie, and their identity can move when durations or packaging change.
Easy A slice is a set of tasks that do not precede one another. It is a precedence-independent layer; genuine simultaneous delivery still depends on resources and other constraints.
Hard An antichain is a set of pairwise incomparable elements. A spine is useful when paired with an antichain partition whose blocks it intersects.
Easy The practical shift is to track the selected chain and the slices it pierces. That makes the structural certificate auditable; any bottleneck claim still needs duration, resource, risk, and assurance evidence.
Hard The certificate is topological rather than narrative: each partition block has at least one spine task, so the chain is visible in every planned parallel layer.

Fishbone structure

In Hollom's terminology, the fishbone is the chain together with an antichain partition whose every block meets that chain: the chain is the trunk and the partition blocks form the ribs. It is not itself a diagnosis of review loops. Repeating gates or review loops are a separate project-controls hypothesis to test against schedule and assurance evidence.

Implementation note

Practical workflow: validate the dependency DAG, derive height layers or verify another proposed antichain partition, test the selected chain against reachability and every partition block, and calculate CPM separately. Revalidate after any topology or slice change; add resource constraints and buffers only in a separate CCPM analysis.

Theory boundary: finite existence follows from height-layer/Mirsky reasoning: a maximum-cardinality chain has one element in each block of a minimum antichain layering. Hollom supplies the spine terminology and establishes important infinite-poset boundaries; this toy finite certificate does not implement his non-constructive infinite proof.

Control interpretation: the certificate proves a structural relationship only. It can motivate a governance, bottleneck, or resource-protection hypothesis, but those decisions require duration, resource, risk, and assurance evidence.
